  The explicit rule — client/learning/combat.txt:23:

  ▎ "Put any uncertain card LAST and never plan around a draw you have not 
  ▎ seen."

  What counts as "uncertain" — client/learning/state.mjs:496, a regex on the
  card's description text, and draw is the first alternative in it. So any card
  whose text contains the word "draw" is uncertain by construction.

  That classification has two consequences:

  - state.mjs:478 — uncertain cards are skipped in the energy pre-budget.
    Permissive; it exists so the validator doesn't reject turns whose arithmetic
    it can't do.
  - executor.mjs:836 → 919 — playing an uncertain card sets barrier: 'card 
    effect requires fresh planning' and breaks the batch. Everything planned
    after it is discarded and the agent replans from fresh state.

  So there is a structural cost asymmetry, and it disfavours draws early: a plan
  is up to eight cards, but a draw card placed first truncates it to one and
  spends a whole decision round-trip (~90s at max reasoning) to get the rest.
  Playing draws later is strictly cheaper in decisions. The agent isn't told to
  prefer or avoid draw cards strategically — it's told where to put them in a
  plan, and the runtime enforces that by stopping there regardless.

  Two things worth flagging as observations, since you asked about unintentional
  nudging:

  1. The regex matches the word, not the effect. /draw/ also fires on "Draw
     Pile", so a card like "Deal damage equal to the cards in your Draw Pile" —
     fully deterministic — is treated as uncertain, forced last, and truncates
     the batch. Deck-size and pile-reference cards get penalised for mentioning
     a pile they only read.
  2. The one library line on the subject is descriptive, not prescriptive:
     meta_strategy/playbook/README.md:28, "Draw and cycling convert leftover
     energy into options." No verdict attached.
